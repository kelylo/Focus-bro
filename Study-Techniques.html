The learner's toolkit

Study less. Remember more.

Use methods that make your brain retrieve, explain, and apply ideas. Choose one technique for the next session—not all of them at once.

01

Active recall

Close the book and pull the answer from memory.

  • Read a small section.
  • Hide it and write what you remember.
  • Check, correct, and repeat tomorrow.
Prompt: “What are the three ideas I could explain without looking?”
02

Spaced repetition

Return to ideas just before they fade.

  • Review today, then tomorrow.
  • Review again after 3, 7, and 14 days.
  • Make cards short: one question, one answer.
Rule: schedule the next review before ending this session.
03

Feynman technique

Explain a concept simply enough for a curious child.

  • Choose one concept.
  • Explain it in plain language.
  • Find the gaps, then simplify again.
If you need jargon to explain it, you probably need another pass.
04

Interleaving

Mix related problem types so you learn when to use each method.

  • Practice two or three related skills.
  • Change the problem type every few questions.
  • Ask: “What signal tells me to use this?”
Finish with one unfamiliar problem to test transfer.
